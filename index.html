Research interests

Models as instruments, not just engines

My work asks how large language models can serve not only as engineering artifacts but as scientific instruments. Much of today's research chases raw performance; I am drawn instead to questions long studied in the social sciences, how ideas spread, how communities form and change, and how technologies develop over time, and to methods that turn modern machine learning loose on large, messy, real-world data to produce interpretable insight into human behavior and innovation.

Alongside this sits a commitment to reliability. A model's confidence should mean something when its answers inform real decisions, so I build uncertainty-aware and robust methods grounded in conformal prediction, calibration, and principled abstention. A PhD is, for me, the right place to bring these threads together into trustworthy AI that bridges machine learning with computational social science and the science of science.

Research Experience

The University of Chicago

Jun 2026 – Present
Research Intern · Knowledge Lab, NSF APTO / Chicago, IL

I build a product-lineage graph over 954K semiconductor designs across 302 chip categories, tracing design descent and cross-manufacturer copying with sentence-transformer embeddings, spec features, and part-number provenance priors in a learned ranker over eight million-plus edges. To measure how commoditized a design space has become, I define a saturation index, the size of a design's calibrated ancestor set, with a distribution-free split-conformal guarantee (0.908 coverage at a 0.90 target, a median of 48 indistinguishable predecessors). Adapting identity-by-descent and identity-by-state from population genetics, I separate in-firm inheritance from cross-firm second-sourcing and trace how dominant designs emerge.

Supervisor: Dr. Julia Koschinsky · Mentor: Dr. Dawoon Jung
